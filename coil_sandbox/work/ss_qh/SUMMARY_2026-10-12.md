# precise_QH coil work, 2026-10-02 → 10-10: configurations, results, and is any arcB2 run publishable?

Written 2026-10-12 as a recap. It covers the session that ran 2026-10-02 → 07 (the fixed single-stage boundary, the
planar optimality diagnosis, and the switch to the new solver) and three follow-on sessions that ran 2026-10-07 → 10
(the boundary × coils matrix, demountable hinges, field quality, and length/coil count).

The headline arcB2 numbers were re-read from each run's `result.json` on 2026-10-12. Everything else is taken from
the write-ups listed in section 4, which hold the detail. B·n always means ⟨|B·n|⟩/⟨|B|⟩ from the sandbox dense
check (`sandbox/common.py: evaluate`); "max" is max |B·n|/⟨|B|⟩. Margins are value/bound (≤ 1 for L, κ, κ_MS;
≥ 1 for d_cc, d_pc), judged against the bounds without backoffs.

## 2. Results and takeaways

### 2.1 Fixed single-stage boundary (`fixed_ss_eq/`)

With the new solver every run converged, with optimality and violation < 1e-6 and every inner solve meeting its
tolerance.

| coils | B·n | max | ÷ XYZ | old solver | outer/inner its | wall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FourierXYZ N6 | 5.192e-4 | 2.12e-3 | 1.00 | 5.231e-4 | 11/94 | 218 s |
| arcB2 M5 | 1.449e-3 | 6.35e-3 | 2.79 | 1.449e-3 | 5/72 | 143 s |
| planar N7 (cold start) | 1.852e-2 | 7.77e-2 | 35.7 | 1.861e-2 (κ infeasible) | 11/453 | 500 s |

* The old arcB2 and XYZ results were already at their optima. The old planar runs had the right B·n but never became
  feasible.
* Viewers: `fixed_ss_eq/view.html` (new solver), `view_oldsolver.html`, and `planar_history.html` (planar
  checkpoints). Tables: `table.md`, `table_oldsolver.md`. Write-up: `fixed_ss_eq/RESULTS.md`.

### 2.2 Why the old solver failed on planar coils (`fixed_ss_eq/RESULTS.md`)

* The trust radius was never binding: the steps sat inside it with a model ratio of about 1.
* The old `lsq-auglag` turns each inequality into a slack variable confined to a box, and handles the box with
  scipy's trust-region-reflective step.
  * Steps left the slack box in 56–100% of trials in every run, arcB2 included. Truncation or reflection then cut the
    stride to about 0.
  * The blocking rows were weakly active: the per-node κ rows near a flat peak, and the C¹ distance penalties, whose
    slacks sat about 1e-38 from their 0 bound.
* Removing the κ lower bound (|κ| ≥ 0 floored slacks at inflection points) raised the early stride from 0.0013 to
  0.22, but did not remove the plateau.
* With κ inactive (κ bound ×2) or dropped, planar converged to the same B·n of about 1.86e-2, with an optimality floor
  of about 5e-4 to 2e-3.
* The slack-free hinge AL (`lsq-auglag-composite`) removes the mechanism: planar converged from the cold start in
  11 outer / 453 inner iterations, 500 s.

## 3. Is any arcB2 run of publishable quality?

**Assessment (2026-10-12): none is publication-ready as a standalone coil result; two are close.** The strongest
publishable material is the comparative story, not a single coilset.

### 3.1 The candidates

| candidate | B·n (max) | certified optimum | field traced | demountable | main weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| free arcB2 on B_arcB2 (`matrix/on_arcB2/arcB2`) | 1.48e-3 (6.5e-3) | yes (opt 6.9e-7, viol 8.0e-7) | yes: QS 1.7e-3 at ρ 0.5, ε_eff ≤ 0.34% | n/a | 111° hinge kinks; κ 0.07% over between nodes |
| level arcB2 with continuation (`joint/cont/refine`) | 2.27e-3 (8.6e-3) | yes (opt 4.1e-7, viol 3.1e-7) | yes: QS 1.95e-3 at ρ 0.5, ε_eff ≤ 0.13% | yes, +7.4 cm | 102° hinge kinks; d_cc only 1.7% clear |
| free arcB2 L ×1.25 (`nc5xyz/arcL125_free`) | 6.1e-4 (2.9e-3) | yes | proxy only | n/a | longer conductor; fixed boundary only (no single stage at this length) |
| level arcB2 L ×1.25 (`nc5xyz/arcL125_level`) | 1.29e-3 (6.0e-3) | yes | proxy only | yes, +4.1 cm | as above; κ 0.1% over |

### 3.2 What a reviewer would raise, most serious first

1. **The hinge corners.**
   * The arcs meet with 102–111° tangent jumps.
   * The κ constraint excludes the corner points, so as optimized these are sharp kinks, which a real conductor cannot
     follow.
   * A paper needs either a finite-radius joint and its field cost, or the existing corner-angle bound (`run_one.py
     --corner-max`, `sandbox/corners.py`; not yet in `run_al.py`) priced as a constraint.
2. **Strict feasibility.**
   * Every arcB2 result reads κ 0.04–0.11% over on the dense check, because the optimizer puts peaks between the 601
     enforcement nodes. Some matrix cells read κ_MS up to 0.6% over.
   * The fix is cheap: a ~0.3% κ/κ_MS backoff and a rerun, about 10 min per stage-2 run.
3. **Length dependence.**
   * Length binds in every run, and 25% more length changes B·n by 2.4×. The bound is borrowed from Gil et al.
     (a-scaled).
   * A credible claim needs a B·n- and QS-versus-length curve (L × 1.0, 1.1, 1.25, 1.5), not one point.
4. **Robustness.**
   * Each case has one start, one set of single-stage weights, and one equilibrium family (precise_QH-derived).
   * A few perturbed starts per flagship case would show the optima are not lucky basins. The QUASR 1328095 cold start
     did fall into a poor basin.
5. **The plasma cost and comparability.**
   * B_arcB2's QS is 3.3× worse than precise_QH at the edge (4.8e-3) and 3.2× at ρ 0.5. ι dropped slightly
     (1.240–1.252 vs precise_QH's 1.252–1.256; still well above the 1.19 floor), and the volume sits at the lower edge
     of its ±2% band. These must be stated.
   * Literature comparisons need the same metric (max |B·n|/B: 6.5e-3 here) and ideally the same boundary and bounds as
     Gil et al. or Jorge, Giuliani & Loizu (arXiv 2406.07830: their planar-coil case is max 1.4e-2, their best
     non-planar single stage 6e-4).
6. **Scope.** Vacuum only; no electromagnetic forces or stress, no finite coil build, no finite beta. This is
   acceptable for a representation study if stated clearly.
7. **The evaluation method's own floor.**
   * The traced-surface pipeline reads well for arcB2 (its coils miss the target by 0.02–0.03 in ρ).
   * For near-perfect coils it over-reads. Report the proxy–traced bracket where relevant (BEST_QS.md §3).

### 3.3 What is already close to publishable as findings

* **Representation-aware single stage.** Restricted coils gain 3–4× from a boundary co-designed for them, and much less
  from a boundary designed for other coils (matrix: planar 0.31× vs 0.76–0.98×; arcB2 0.27× vs 0.43–0.57×). A
  boundary designed for arcB2 is also coil-friendly in general.
* **The price of demountability.**
  * Level hinges per coil cost 1.5× free arcB2 (with continuation).
  * A single joint plane costs 4.3× on QH and is ruled out geometrically for QUASR QH devices.
  * QA devices only make a joint plane cheap at low ι.
* **The solver result.** The slack-based AL fails on weakly active constraints; the slack-free hinge AL converges.
  This is the DESC branch's contribution rather than the sandbox's.
* All of this rests on certified optima (optimality and violation < 1e-6) and, for two arcB2 cases, traced coil
  fields.

### 3.4 Shortest path to a paper-quality arcB2 result

1. Rerun the two flagship cases (free on B_arcB2; level with continuation) with a ~0.3% κ/κ_MS backoff.
2. Add a corner-angle bound to `run_al.py` (port `CoilCornerAngle` as AL rows), and price it at a few angles.
3. Sweep length L × {1.0, 1.1, 1.25, 1.5}: stage 2 on fixed boundaries first, then single stage at the chosen length.
4. Run 3–5 perturbed starts per flagship case.
5. Report, for each: mean and max B·n, margins, traced coil-field QS and ε_eff, and the boundary's QS/ι/volume cost.

Cost: about 10 min per stage-2 run and about 1 h per single stage (a continuation run is somewhat longer).
